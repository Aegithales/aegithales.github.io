Welcome to my website!

Sometimes reading about the grand unsolved problems in science is more engaging than reading papers with minor contributions to existing theories. I somehow got hooked on this ‘unsolved problems’ search because even review papers don’t list them. It is not that they are hidden or denied - we just don’t talk about them.

So here is my list of ten or twenty-something unsolved scientific problems that I find important and profound:

  1. Abiogenesis: how life arose on Earth almost instantly after oceans formed, as LUCA (the Last Universal Common Ancestor) already contained the fully developed transcriptional / translational machinery?

  2. Evolution: why species are so stable? If mutations and genetic drifting is the primary way of evolution, achieving swarm intelligence why don’t we see that ‘swarm’ of variants, each diverging from its ancestors a little, exploring the fitness landscape?

  3. True infinity of the cosmos (in space): is there a way to prove it? Does it have observable effects? Hint: it does…

  4. Extinction: while species often disappear because of a change in environment, or because of competition from other species for the same resource, there must be also an intrinsic mechanism for extinction, practically limiting the

  5. The side-branch complexity problem: it is expected to see evolutionary steps that leads to humans - otherwise we won’t be here to make this observation. But would not this ‘anthropic’ selection result in a narrow, nonbranching tree-of-life? Why do we have a diverse biosphere with millions of strange species? Why do we see probability amplification on the side-branches, not needed for humans?

  6. Morphogenesis: how can be the development of adult form so unerring, so accurate, unaffected by noise, not accumulating errors, even though it has no feedback on the shape and size and structure of organs and limbs?

  7. How instintive behaviours are encoded in the genome? For example, spiders don’t learn how to weave orb webs, they just know it…

  8. Perception is the process that incrementally build / maintain an internal representation of our surroundings, by processing sensory (mostly visual) input.

    What is the neural architecture that is capable to integrate, not only the visuals, but also prior knowledge of familiar objects, persons, and locations into this representation?

  9. Long term (semantic, episodic) memory vs transient, ‘working’ memory: how the brain holds an interpretation of a scene, or a sentence, or a narrative, as patched together from pieces on perception and references to long-term memory?

  10. Intent: how intent is formed? How is it combined with perception to select and control the action?

    How innate goals / desires (like mating, survival, etc) restrict or give rise to intent and actions?

  11. How to deliberately control thought? How to rehearse actions / plans in the mind? How to compose and hold a mental image?

  12. How mental disorders, like depression, psychosis, etc can be modeled?

    How can we model neurodevelopmental disorders, like intellectual disability, motor disorders, etc?
